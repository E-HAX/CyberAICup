# OKF reference bundle
Part 1: official text for all 41 checking points, with pointers to the
policy sections (Part 2) that govern each. Part 2: every cited policy
section's text, included exactly once even if multiple CPs cite it.

## Part 1 — Checking-point index


### CP1 (Element-1 — 1.1 Export operations)
**Official text:** The establishment is operating within its registered operations and, where applicable, its registered functions to prepare plants or plant products for export.
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-1.md, policy/section_4-5.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-1 states that an establishment is registered for 'a kind of export operations in relation to a kind of prescribed plants or plant products', and section 4-5 prescribes that the conditions of registration likewise apply 'for a kind of export operations in relation to a kind of prescribed plants or plant products', together establishing that lawful operation at the establishment is bounded by the specific export operations for which it is registered.

### CP2 (Element-1 — 1.1 Export operations)
**Official text:** At the time the export operations are carried out, the registration of the establishment must not be suspended in relation to those operations or functions.
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-18.md, policy/section_4-19.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Sections 4-18 and 4-19 prescribe the circumstances and grounds on which the registration of an establishment may be suspended (at the occupier's request or by the Secretary), which is the legal basis for the requirement that export operations not be carried out while the registration covering those operations or functions is suspended.

### CP3 (Element-1 — 1.2 Plans and specifications)
**Official text:** the area of the premises that is registered for export operations
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-1.md, policy/section_4-2.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-1 ties registration to a specific establishment being registered for a kind of export operations, and section 4-2(2)-(4) requires that establishment to have the buildings, equipment, facilities and services necessary for those operations, together defining the registered area of premises that must be documented as the basis of registration.

### CP4 (Element-1 — 1.2 Plans and specifications)
**Official text:** all important features of the registered area and adjoining sites, including the buildings, facilities and services to conduct the export operations
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-2.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-2(2) and (4) require an establishment to have the buildings, equipment, facilities and services necessary for export operations to be carried out compliantly, which is the substantive requirement that plans/specifications documenting the important features of the registered area and adjoining buildings, facilities and services must demonstrate.

### CP5 (Element-1 — 1.2 Plans and specifications)
**Official text:** the layout of the areas where export operations occur, including all permanent fixtures, and layout of equipment used to prepare plants and plant products
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-2.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-2(4) requires the establishment and its equipment and facilities to be designed and constructed to provide suitable assessment areas, adequate lighting/ventilation, effective cleaning, minimised infestation/contamination risk, waste disposal, safe treatment, and sample-taking capability, which is the layout-governing text that plans showing permanent fixtures and equipment layout must reflect.

### CP6 (Element-1 — 1.3 Establishment changes and alterations)
**Official text:** Where required, notification has been provided to the department of relevant changes to the registration, including the business structure, persons in management and control, trading name, address and contact details.
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-23.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-23(c)(i) identifies an application under section 120 of the Act 'to vary the registration, or the particulars relating to the registration, of the establishment' as the formal mechanism through which relevant changes to a registered establishment (such as its business structure, trading name, address and contact details) must be notified to and recorded by the department.

### CP7 (Element-1 — 1.3 Establishment changes and alterations)
**Official text:** Where required, notices of alterations, extensions and rearrangements to the registered establishment that may affect compliance with the conditions of registration have been submitted to and approved by the department.
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-6.md, policy/section_4-16.md, policy/section_4-23.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-6's note states that alterations of a registered establishment (other than those prescribed by section 4-16) must not be made unless approved under subsection 120(2) of the Act; section 4-16 prescribes which alterations do NOT require approval (those not affecting compliance with the conditions of registration), implying by exclusion that alterations, extensions or rearrangements that may affect compliance do require approval; and section 4-23(c)(ii) confirms that 'approve an alteration of the establishment' is the relevant application type under section 120 of the Act, together forming the basis for the requirement that such notices be submitted to and approved by the department.

### CP8 (Element-2 — 2.1 Buildings, equipment, facilities and service)
**Official text:** The establishment has the buildings, equipment, facilities and services required for the type of export operations being conducted.
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-2.md, policy/section_4-6.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-2(2) requires an establishment to have the buildings, equipment, facilities and services necessary to carry out the export operations concerned, and section 4-6 requires that this section 4-2 requirement continue to be met after the establishment is registered, so together they are the basis for CP8's ongoing check of whether the establishment has what is required for the type of export operations conducted.

### CP9 (Element-2 — 2.1 Buildings, equipment, facilities and service)
**Official text:** There is adequate lighting for the export operations being conducted.
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-2.md, policy/section_4-6.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-2(4)(a)(ii) requires the establishment and its equipment/facilities to be designed and constructed to provide adequate lighting (and ventilation), and section 4-6 carries this requirement forward as an ongoing obligation on the registered establishment, which is what CP9 checks.

### CP10 (Element-2 — 2.1 Buildings, equipment, facilities and service)
**Official text:** There is hand washing and toilet facilities at the establishment.
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-2.md, policy/section_4-6.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-2(3) expressly requires an establishment to have handwashing and toilet facilities, and section 4-6 requires this section 4-2 requirement to continue to be met once the establishment is registered, matching CP10's check that such facilities exist at the establishment.

### CP11 (Element-2 — 2.2 Design and construction of the establishment)
**Official text:** The establishment is designed, constructed and maintained to allow for the effective cleaning of buildings, equipment and facilities used to conduct export operations.
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-2.md, policy/section_4-6.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-2(4)(b) requires the establishment and its equipment/facilities to be designed and constructed to facilitate effective cleaning, and section 4-6 requires that this design/construction requirement continue to be met (i.e. be maintained) after registration, which is exactly the 'designed, constructed and maintained... effective cleaning' language of CP11.

### CP12 (Element-2 — 2.2 Design and construction of the establishment)
**Official text:** The establishment is designed, constructed and maintained to minimise the contamination, infestation and pest harbourage during export operations.
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-2.md, policy/section_4-6.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-2(4)(c) requires the establishment and its equipment/facilities to be designed and constructed to minimise the possibility of infestation or contamination of prescribed plants or plant products, and section 4-6 requires this to continue to be met after registration (i.e. be maintained), directly underpinning CP12's 'designed, constructed and maintained... minimise contamination, infestation and pest harbourage' wording.

### CP13 (Element-2 — 2.2 Design and construction of the establishment)
**Official text:** The establishment is designed, constructed and maintained to provide the disposal of all waste material, including solids and liquids, in an efficient and hygienic manner.
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-2.md, policy/section_4-6.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-2(4)(d) requires the establishment and its equipment/facilities to be designed and constructed to provide for disposal of all waste material (including liquids and solids) in an appropriate and hygienic manner, and section 4-6 requires that requirement to continue to be met (maintained) once registered, matching CP13's waste-disposal design/construction/maintenance check.

### CP14 (Element-2 — 2.3 Inspection areas (including sampling points))
**Official text:** The inspection areas, equipment and facilities are designed, constructed and maintained to allow for the collection, inspection and analysis of samples by an Authorised Officer for the type of prescribed plant or plant products. This includes, but is not limited to: inspection bench, where applicable.
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-2.md, policy/section_4-6.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-2(4)(a)(i) and (f) require the establishment to be designed and constructed to provide areas suitable for assessments of prescribed plants or plant products and to allow samples to be taken (with subsection (5) extending this to testing/analysis facilities), and section 4-6 requires these design/construction requirements to continue to be met (maintained) after registration, together forming the basis for CP14's check of inspection areas, equipment and facilities including inspection benches.

### CP15 (Element-2 — 2.4 Screening)
**Official text:** manage the risk of contamination by large contaminants
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-9.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-9(2)(a) provides that, if screening of the plants or plant products is carried out at the establishment, it must be carried out in a way that is appropriate to manage the risk of contamination by large contaminants, which is the verbatim source of CP15's obligation.

### CP16 (Element-2 — 2.4 Screening)
**Official text:** ensure that any large contaminants are removed from the plants or plant products
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-9.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-9(2)(b) provides that screening carried out at the establishment must be carried out in a way that is appropriate to ensure that any large contaminants are removed from the plants or plant products, which is the verbatim source of CP16's obligation.

### CP17 (Element-3 — 3.1 Systems of controls – hygiene and waste control)
**Official text:** the system of controls to manage hygiene
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-3.md, policy/section_4-7A.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-3(3) requires a system of controls to be in place at the establishment to ensure the conditions in section 4-7A are complied with, and section 4-7A(2)(a) is the specific condition requiring the establishment to be kept in a clean and hygienic condition, so together they are the basis for requiring a system of controls that manages hygiene.

### CP18 (Element-3 — 3.1 Systems of controls – hygiene and waste control)
**Official text:** the system of controls to manage waste control and disposal
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-3.md, policy/section_4-7A.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-3(3) requires a system of controls to ensure compliance with section 4-7A, and section 4-7A(2)(d) is the specific condition requiring waste at the establishment to be appropriately treated and disposed of, so together they establish the obligation to have a system of controls for waste control and disposal.

### CP19 (Element-3 — 3.1 Systems of controls – hygiene and waste control)
**Official text:** The system of controls is effective for the type of export operations and activities conducted at the establishment.
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-3.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-3(3) requires that there be a system of controls in place at the establishment in relation to the export operations and plants or plant products for which it is registered, which is the source of the general requirement that the system of controls be effective for the type of export operations and activities conducted.

### CP20 (Element-3 — 3.2 Systems of controls – pest control)
**Official text:** the system of controls to manage pests and contaminants including but not limited to rodent control (for example, bait stations)
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-3.md, policy/section_4-7A.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-3(3) requires a system of controls to ensure compliance with section 4-7A, and section 4-7A(1)(b) and 4-7A(2)(b) are the specific conditions requiring that prescribed plants or plant products not be infested by pests or contaminated and that pests and contaminants be controlled appropriately (which encompasses measures such as rodent bait stations), so together they impose the obligation for a system of controls managing pests and contaminants.

### CP21 (Element-3 — 3.2 Systems of controls – pest control)
**Official text:** The system of controls is effective for the type of export operations and activities conducted at the establishment.
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-3.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-3(3) is the same general requirement for an effective system of controls in relation to the export operations and activities conducted at the establishment that underlies CP19, applied here to the pest-control system of controls addressed by CP20.

### CP22 (Element-3 — 3.3 Record keeping – hygiene, waste and pest control)
**Official text:** At least 2 years of records are retained to demonstrate compliance with the system of controls in place to manage hygiene, waste control and pest control.
**Governed by:** policy/section_11-7.md, policy/section_4-7A.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 11-7(1)(b) and (2) require the occupier of a registered establishment to retain, for at least 2 years, each record relevant to showing compliance with the conditions of registration, and section 4-7A is the substantive condition of registration (hygiene, waste and pest control) that those records must demonstrate compliance with.

### CP23 (Element-3 — 3.3 Record keeping – hygiene, waste and pest control)
**Official text:** Records must: be in English; include the date of action; be accurate, legible and able to be audited.
**Governed by:** policy/section_11-2.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 11-2(1) directly prescribes that a record required to be retained under Chapter 11 must be in English, dated, and accurate, legible and able to be audited, matching the format requirements stated in CP23.

### CP24 (Element-3 — 3.4 Establishment condition – hygiene and waste control)
**Official text:** All areas of the establishment are operating and/or maintained in a clean and hygienic condition appropriate for the type of export operations and type of plant or plant products.
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-7A.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-7A(2)(a) directly requires a registered establishment to be kept in a clean and hygienic condition appropriate for the export operations carried out and the kind of plants or plant products, which is the exact obligation described by CP24.

### CP25 (Element-3 — 3.5 Establishment condition – pest control)
**Official text:** All areas of the establishment are operating and/or maintained in a condition that ensures prescribed goods are not, or not likely to be infested by pests or be contaminated.
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-7A.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-7A(1)(b) requires the establishment to be kept in a condition that ensures prescribed plants and plant products are not, or not likely to be, infested by pests or contaminated, and 4-7A(2)(b) requires pests and contaminants to be controlled appropriately, directly matching CP25's condition-of-establishment obligation.

### CP26 (Element-3 — 3.5 Establishment condition – pest control)
**Official text:** Where applicable, pest control stations and traps (for example, rodent bait stations) are fit for purpose and in good working order.
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-7A.md, policy/section_4-6.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-7A(2)(b) requires pests and contaminants at the establishment to be controlled in a manner appropriate for the export operations (which includes devices such as bait stations and traps being fit for purpose), and section 4-6 makes the equipment and facilities requirements of section 4-2 (including that equipment be adequate to ensure the requirements of the Act are met) continue to apply to a registered establishment, together supporting the obligation that pest-control equipment be fit for purpose and in good working order.

### CP27 (Element-3 — 3.6 Chemical storage)
**Official text:** contaminate prescribed plant or plant products
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-7A.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-7A(2)(c) requires toxic substances (including fumigants, rodenticides and insecticides) at the establishment to be managed and stored appropriately, and 4-7A(1)(b) requires that prescribed plants or plant products not be, or not likely to be, contaminated, together imposing the chemical-storage obligation aimed at preventing contamination of the plants or plant products described by CP27.

### CP28 (Element-3 — 3.6 Chemical storage)
**Official text:** contaminate areas where prescribed plant or plant products are prepared
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-7A.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-7A(2)(c) requires toxic substances to be managed and stored appropriately, and 4-7A(1)(a) requires the establishment to be kept in a condition appropriate for the export operations carried out there, together imposing the chemical-storage obligation aimed at preventing contamination of the areas where prescribed plants or plant products are prepared, as described by CP28.

### CP29 (Element-4 — 4.1 System of controls – product traceability and integrity)
**Official text:** from the property (if any) from which they were transferred to receival into the establishment
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-7B.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-7B(a) expressly requires that prescribed plants and plant products prepared at a registered establishment must be traced 'from the property (if any) from which they were transferred to the registered establishment', which is exactly the receival-into-the-establishment leg of traceability that CP29 checks.

### CP30 (Element-4 — 4.1 System of controls – product traceability and integrity)
**Official text:** while they are at the registered establishment
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-7B.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-7B(b) requires that the plants or plant products must be traced 'while they are at the registered establishment', directly imposing the on-site traceability obligation described by CP30.

### CP31 (Element-4 — 4.1 System of controls – product traceability and integrity)
**Official text:** to the next premises which they are transferred to
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-7B.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-7B(c) requires that the plants or plant products must be traced 'to the premises to which they are transferred from the registered establishment', which is the outbound-transfer leg of traceability that CP31 checks.

### CP32 (Element-4 — 4.1 System of controls – product traceability and integrity)
**Official text:** the systems of controls to maintain traceability and integrity of prescribed plant and plant products (e.g., labelling, record keeping)
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-3.md, policy/section_4-7B.md, policy/section_4-8.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-3(3) requires a 'system of controls' to be in place at the establishment to ensure the traceability condition in section 4-7B (and other conditions) is complied with, while sections 4-7B and 4-8 are the substantive traceability and integrity obligations that CP32's system of controls (e.g. labelling, record keeping) must be designed to maintain.

### CP33 (Element-4 — 4.1 System of controls – product traceability and integrity)
**Official text:** a product flow chart that demonstrates the movement and maintenance of product integrity through the registered establishment
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-3.md, policy/section_4-7B.md, policy/section_4-8.md
**Why these sections govern it:** There is no separate 'product flow chart' provision; the flow chart is documentary evidence of compliance with the system-of-controls requirement in section 4-3(3) and the underlying traceability (4-7B) and integrity (4-8) obligations it must demonstrate movement against and integrity maintenance for through the establishment.

### CP34 (Element-4 — 4.1 System of controls – product traceability and integrity)
**Official text:** the system of controls to manage goods that fail inspection – including blending, rejections, treatments and representations of failed products, where applicable
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-9.md, policy/section_9-11.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-9(3)-(4) directly governs blending, prohibiting blending of plants or plant products that failed assessment due to pest infestation or animal carcase/waste, and section 9-11 governs reassessment and required treatment of plants or plant products after a failed assessment, together forming the legal basis for controls over blending, rejections, treatments and re-presentation of failed goods that CP34 checks.

### CP35 (Element-4 — 4.2 System of controls – phytosanitary security)
**Official text:** maintaining risk for contamination or infestation
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-7A.md, policy/section_4-3.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-7A(1)(b) and (2)(b) require the establishment to be kept in a condition, and pests/contaminants to be controlled, so that prescribed plants and plant products are not, or not likely to be, infested or contaminated, and section 4-3(3) requires a system of controls to ensure this condition is complied with, matching CP35's requirement to maintain/manage contamination and infestation risk.

### CP36 (Element-4 — 4.2 System of controls – phytosanitary security)
**Official text:** minimising the risk of substitution (switching of goods)
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-8.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-8 requires that 'the integrity of prescribed plants or plant products prepared at a registered establishment must be ensured while the plants or plant products are at the establishment', and product integrity is the concept that covers preventing unauthorised switching/substitution of goods described in CP36.

### CP37 (Element-4 — 4.2 System of controls – phytosanitary security)
**Official text:** packaging requirements (where applicable)
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-11.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-11 sets out the requirements for packages used for export (cleanliness, suitability, strength, integrity-preserving use, and meeting importing country requirements), which is precisely the packaging-requirements obligation CP37 checks.

### CP38 (Element-4 — 4.3 Record Keeping - traceability)
**Official text:** At least 2 years of traceability records are retained; including but not limited to receival, product movement, treatment, transfer records, dispatch, and transport unit approval records, where applicable.
**Governed by:** policy/section_11-7.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 11-7 requires the occupier of a registered establishment to retain, for at least 2 years, each document relevant to showing compliance with the applicable requirements of the Act (including registration conditions such as traceability), which is the statutory basis for the 2-year retention of receival, movement, treatment, transfer, dispatch and transport-unit-approval records that CP38 checks.

### CP39 (Element-4 — 4.3 Record Keeping - traceability)
**Official text:** At least 2 years of records for prescribed goods that have been rejected and/or failed export inspection are retained; including but not limited to records of blending, treatments and re-presentation of goods.
**Governed by:** policy/section_11-7.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 11-7 imposes the same 2-year retention duty on the occupier for all documents relevant to demonstrating compliance, which extends to records of blending, treatments and re-presentation of plants or plant products that failed export inspection as covered by CP39.

### CP40 (Element-4 — 4.3 Record Keeping - traceability)
**Official text:** Records must: be in English; include the date of action; be accurate, legible and able to be audited.
**Governed by:** policy/section_11-2.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 11-2(1) requires that a record retained under Chapter 11 must be in English, dated, and accurate, legible and able to be audited, which is a verbatim match to the record-quality requirements CP40 checks.

### CP41 (Element-4 — 4.4 Importing country requirements)
**Official text:** Where applicable, all importing country requirements relating to the export operations carried out at a registered establishment in relation to prescribed plants or plant products and the plants or plant products must be met.
**Governed by:** policy/section_4-7.md
**Why these sections govern it:** Section 4-7 states that 'all importing country requirements relating to the export operations carried out at a registered establishment in relation to prescribed plants or plant products and the plants or plant products must be met', which is a verbatim match to CP41's requirement.

# Section 11-2 — General requirements for records

11-2  General requirements for records 
 
(1) A record that is required to be retained under this Part in relation to plants or 
plant products must be: 
 
(a) in English; and 
 
(b) if the record was required to be in another language to meet importing 
country requirements—in that other language; and 
 
(c) dated; and 
 
(d) accurate, legible and able to be audited. 
 
(2) If a person is required to retain a document under this Part, the person is taken to 
have complied with the requirement if: 
 
(a) the person is required, under a law of the Commonwealth or a State or 
Territory or in accordance with ordinary commercial practice, to give the 
document to another person; and 
 
(b) the person gives the document to the other person as required; and 
 
(c) the person retains a copy of the document.

# Section 11-7 — Records to be retained by occupier of registered establishment

11-7  Records to be retained by occupier of registered establishment 
 
(1) The occupier of an establishment that is registered for a kind of export operations 
in relation to a kind of prescribed plants or plant products must retain each 
document: 
 
(a) that is made by the occupier or that comes into the occupier’s possession; 
and 
 
(b) that is relevant to showing whether the occupier has complied, or is 
complying, with the applicable requirements of the Act (including whether 
the conditions of the registration of the establishment have been, and are 
being, complied with). 
 
(2) The occupier of the registered establishment must retain each record referred to 
in subsection (1) for at least 2 years starting on the day the record is made by the 
occupier or comes into the occupier’s possession (as the case may be).

# Section 4-1 — Purpose of this Part

4-1  Purpose of this Part 
 
 For the purposes of paragraphs 112(2)(c) and (f) of the Act, this Part prescribes 
requirements that must be met for the establishment to be registered for a kind of 
export operations in relation to a kind of prescribed plants or plant products. 
Note: 
The requirements in this Part also apply in relation to an application to renew the 
registration of the establishment (see section 4-15).

# Section 4-11 — Requirements for packages for export

4-11  Requirements for packages for export 
 
(1) This section applies in relation to a registered establishment where prescribed 
plants or plant products are packed into packages for export. 
 
(2) If the packages are intended to be used more than once and they have been used 
previously, the packages must be cleaned and reconditioned before each 
subsequent use. In any other case, the packages must be unused and clean. 
 
(3) The packages must: 
 
(a) be appropriate for the plants or plant products to be packed in them; and 
 
(b) be used in a manner that will ensure the integrity of the plants or plant 
products packed in them. 
 
(4) The packages must be sufficiently strong to withstand handling ordinarily 
occurring before and during transport, including transport to their final overseas 
destination. 
 
(5) The packages must meet relevant importing country requirements.

# Section 4-16 — Alterations for which approval is not required

4-16  Alterations for which approval is not required 
 
 For the purposes of subsection 122(2) of the Act, an alteration of a registered 
establishment is prescribed if it does not affect compliance with the conditions of 
registration of the establishment.

# Section 4-18 — Circumstances in which occupier may request suspension of registration

4-18  Circumstances in which occupier may request suspension of registration 
 
 For the purposes of subsection 125(2) of the Act, the circumstances in which the 
occupier of a registered establishment may make a request under 
subsection 125(1) of the Act are as follows: 
 
(a) a circumstance has arisen that prevents, or will prevent, a kind of export 
operations being carried out at the establishment; 
 
(b) the occupier considers that it will not be possible or practicable for a kind 
of export operations to be carried out at the establishment for any other 
reason.

# Section 4-19 — Other grounds for suspension of registration

4-19  Other grounds for suspension of registration 
 
 For the purposes of paragraph 127(1)(k) of the Act, the following grounds for 
suspending the registration of a registered establishment are prescribed: 
 
(a) the occupier of the establishment, or a person who manages or controls 
export operations at the establishment, failed to provide facilities and 
assistance to an assessor for the purpose of enabling an assessment of 
prescribed plants or plant products to be carried out at the establishment; 
 
(b) the occupier of the establishment, or a person who manages or controls 
export operations at the establishment, failed to comply with a request 
made by an assessor for the purpose of enabling an assessment of 
prescribed plants or plant products to be carried out at the establishment; 
 
(c) if the registration of the establishment covers export operations in relation 
to prescribed plants or plant products for human consumption—the 
occupier of the establishment failed to comply with applicable State and 
Territory laws relating to food handling in carrying out those export 
operations; 
 
(d) the occupier of the establishment, or a person who manages or controls 
export operations at the establishment, failed to comply with section 410 of 
the Act when taking, testing or analysing samples at the establishment.

# Section 4-2 — Construction, equipment and facilities

4-2  Construction, equipment and facilities 
Application of this section 
 
(1) The requirements prescribed by this section apply in relation to all 
establishments. 
Buildings, equipment, facilities and services 
 
(2) An establishment must have the buildings, equipment, facilities and services that 
are necessary to ensure that export operations in relation to prescribed plants or 
plant products can be carried out in a way that will ensure that the requirements 
of the Act are complied with. 
 
(3) An establishment must have handwashing and toilet facilities. 
Design and construction—general 
 
(4) An establishment and its equipment and facilities must be designed and

# Section 4-23 — Application of this Part

4-23  Application of this Part 
 
 This Part applies in relation to the following applications: 
 
(a) an application under section 111 of the Act to register an establishment for 
a kind of export operations in relation to a kind of prescribed plants or 
plant products; 
 
(b) an application under section 116 of the Act to renew the registration of an 
establishment for a kind of export operations in relation to a kind of 
prescribed plants or plant products; 
 
(c) an application under section 120 of the Act to do any of the following in 
relation to an establishment that is registered for a kind of export 
operations in relation to a kind of prescribed plants or plant products: 
 
(i) vary the registration, or the particulars relating to the registration, of 
the establishment; 
 
(ii) approve an alteration of the establishment; 
 
(iii) vary the conditions of the registration of the establishment.

# Section 4-5 — Purpose of this Part

4-5  Purpose of this Part 
 
 For the purposes of paragraph 113(1)(b) of the Act, this Part prescribes 
conditions of the registration of an establishment for a kind of export operations 
in relation to a kind of prescribed plants or plant products. 
Note 1: 
If the registration of the establishment is renewed, these conditions also apply in 
relation to the renewed registration of the establishment (see paragraph 118(b) of the 
Act). 
Note 2: 
The occupier of a registered establishment may commit an offence or be liable to a 
civil penalty if a condition of the registration of the establishment is contravened (see 
section 144 of the Act). 
Note 3: 
The occupier of a registered establishment must retain certain records for at least 2 
years (see section 11-7 of this instrument).

that could infest plants or plant products, at the establishment.

# Section 9-11 — Reassessment after failed assessment

9-11  Reassessment after failed assessment 
 
(1) If plants or plant products fail an assessment (the failed assessment), the plants 
or plant products must not be presented for another assessment (a reassessment) 
unless the reason for the failed assessment has been rectified. 
 
(2) If the reason for the failed assessment was because pests or contaminants above 
the applicable tolerance level were detected on the plants or plant products or in 
or on the flowpath for the plants or plant products, the relevant person for the 
failed assessment must ensure that the plants or plant products or the flowpath 
(depending on where the pests or contaminants were detected) is treated using a 
method that: 
 
(a) is effective to reduce the pests or contaminants to no higher than the 
applicable tolerance level; and 
 
(b) meets importing country requirements relating to the plants or plant 
products. 
 
(3) If the plants or plant products are required to be treated under subsection (2) and 
the exporter of the plants or plant products is not the relevant person for the 
failed assessment, the exporter of the plants or plant products must agree to the 
method of treatment to be used. 
Note: 
The relevant person for the failed assessment may be the occupier of the registered 
establishment where operations to prepare the plants or plant products for export were 
carried out.
